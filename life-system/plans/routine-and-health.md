# Weekly routine, gym and food

## Scope

This is a general low-intensity starter template, not a personalized medical, training or nutrition prescription. It uses adult public-health guidance but does not assume your age, fitness, health, schedule, food preferences, budget or goal. Personalize only after those details are known. Keep health details in private notes or conversation, never in the public task feed.

## Starter movement week

| Day | Movement | Work/life rhythm |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mon | Comfortable walk, 15–20 min | Pick one work priority; set a reasonable stop time |
| Tue | Easy full-body strength, about 15–20 min | Keep the effort light and learn form |
| Wed | Comfortable walk, 15–20 min | One career or project step |
| Thu | Rest or gentle mobility | Protect recovery and a consistent sleep window |
| Fri | Easy full-body strength, about 15–20 min | Leave plenty of effort in reserve |
| Sat | Comfortable walk, 20–25 min | AI World 360 creation block |
| Sun | Rest | Review the week and plan the next one |

If you have been inactive, begin with shorter, comfortable sessions. Build duration and repetitions gradually; do not try to reach the full adult activity target in the first week. WHO guidance for adults is 150–300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity weekly, plus muscle-strengthening activity on at least two days. The schedule above is a starting point below that target, with a gradual build only as tolerated.

### Easy strength-session structure

1. Warm up with a few minutes of easy walking or movement.
2. Choose comfortable versions of these movement patterns: chair sit-to-stand or leg press; wall push-up or chest press; seated/band row; supported calf raise or a comfortable hip movement.
3. Start with one light, controlled set of about five repetitions per movement, resting as needed. Increase repetitions slowly only when the movement feels comfortable and form stays controlled. Ask a qualified gym instructor to check equipment setup and form.
4. Stop if an exercise causes sharp or increasing pain. Stop the session and seek appropriate care for chest pain, faintness, or unusual breathlessness. If you have a condition, injury, recent surgery, pregnancy, or clinician-set restrictions, get suitable professional guidance before following a new routine.

## Flexible Indian meal pattern

Use these as meal ideas, not fixed portions or calorie targets. Choose foods that fit your preferences, allergies, health needs, budget and cooking time. A useful meal pattern is vegetables/greens plus whole grains or millets plus pulses/beans, with suitable protein and fruit across the day. Add nuts/seeds or plain curd/yogurt if suitable. Prefer variety; keep added sugar, excess salt and heavily processed foods limited. No supplement or restrictive diet is required by this draft.

| Meal | Choose an option |
| --- | --- |
| Breakfast | Idli with sambar and fruit; **or** vegetable poha/upma with peanuts or another suitable protein; **or** oats/dalia with fruit and nuts/seeds. |
| Lunch | Rice/roti/millet with dal, rajma or chole and vegetables/greens; **or** the same grain-and-vegetable base with eggs, fish, chicken, paneer or tofu according to preference. |
| Snack, if wanted | Fruit with roasted chana; **or** plain curd/yogurt; **or** a small handful of suitable nuts/seeds. |
| Dinner | Vegetables/greens with a grain and dal/beans; **or** eggs, fish, chicken, paneer or tofu according to preference. |

On gym days, eat your usual balanced meals around your schedule; timing can be adjusted after your work hours and appetite are known. Drink according to thirst unless a clinician has given you different fluid guidance. These examples do not set weight-loss, weight-gain, protein or calorie targets.

## Personalization checklist

Before turning this into your actual weekly plan, record privately: fitness goal; current activity and training experience; gym/equipment access; work and sleep windows; preferred training days; vegetarian/egg/non-vegetarian preference; foods avoided; allergies; relevant health conditions, medication or injuries; cooking time and budget; and any clinician advice. If you prefer, share only the constraints that affect the plan.

For two weeks, optionally track completed movement, energy, sleep consistency and any discomfort. Adjust one schedule item at a time. Get a clinician or registered dietitian's guidance for medical conditions, injuries, major diet changes or condition-specific targets.
