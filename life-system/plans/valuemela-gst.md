## First decide what ValueMela does

Do not choose a GST registration route until the actual legal entity and sales flow are clear. Record answers in a secure place, not in this public-capable task tracker:

- Which legal entity operates ValueMela (sole proprietorship, partnership/LLP, company or another form), and in which state is it established?
- Does ValueMela sell only its own goods/services, facilitate third-party sellers on a platform, or do both?
- Who owns/operates the online platform, issues each invoice, collects the buyer’s payment, and supplies/ships each item?
- Which goods/services will be supplied, which may be exempt or taxable, and where are inventory and business premises located?
- Will sales be intra-state or inter-state, through ValueMela’s own site, another marketplace, or both? What turnover and launch date are expected?
- What is the proposed invoicing and seller-onboarding model, including whether any e-commerce tax collection obligations may apply?

Do not assume registration is or is not already required; have the practitioner determine the trigger and any missed application/return/payment deadlines from the facts.
- Which orders, deposits, dispatches, returns or cancellations may occur before a GSTIN is granted? Ask the practitioner to determine the liability/effective date, transaction-specific treatment, seller of record and any applicable revised-invoice procedure. This checklist does not direct a blanket halt to all sales or dispatches. An unregistered person must not collect an amount as GST or issue a GST tax invoice; do not implement a pre-GSTIN tax-collection or invoicing workaround without written practitioner advice.
- For every stock or fulfilment location, who owns the inventory, who controls the premises, whether it is a third-party/ECO warehouse, whether goods are stored across state borders, and which legal entity invoices and dispatches from it?
- Who prepares and checks transport documents and any e-way bill before movement? Identify consignor/consignee/transporter responsibilities, dispatch locations, interstate movements, relevant value/document details, state-specific rules and exemptions; the practitioner must confirm applicability for the actual transaction.

### New e-commerce warehouse option to assess (effective 1 November 2026)

Notification 05/2026-Central Tax inserted CGST Rule 14B. A supplier already registered in a home State may, subject to conditions, seek a same-PAN registration in another State without physical presence there by declaring a consenting section 52 e-commerce operator's warehouse as the principal place of business. Conditions include supplying goods through that operator, the combined central, state/UT and integrated output-tax liability on these supplies to registered persons (other than persons on the same PAN) does not exceed ₹2,50,000 per month, operator consent, and biometric Aadhaar authentication / document checks and physical verification at the home-State premises. This could matter if ValueMela sells goods through such an operator and holds inventory in its warehouse; it is not a general exemption or automatic route. The CA must also map ongoing record/location/consent requirements, destination registration dependencies, changes that require amendments, ceiling breach or out-of-channel supplies, and withdrawal/cancellation steps (including REG-32 where applicable). Have the CA confirm ValueMela's eligibility, the corrected Gazette wording, portal implementation and practical obligations before choosing this route.

Have an Indian GST practitioner/CA confirm the registration trigger, state-wise registrations, business classification, product tax treatment, e-commerce provisions and scheme eligibility against the actual model and current law. CBIC’s portal FAQs distinguish an electronic-commerce operator from a business selling only its own products and from suppliers selling through an operator; do not infer ValueMela’s classification from its brand name or website alone.

### Written registration-route decision matrix

Ask the practitioner to document, for each relevant State/UT and the actual liability date, the facts and current authority for CGST Act sections 22, 23, 24 and 25: aggregate turnover calculation by PAN; applicable thresholds, exemptions and notifications; interstate supplies; compulsory-registration categories; separate state registrations and additional places; and any marketplace/TCS or other special provisions. Do not hardcode a threshold or infer liability from a storefront label. Record the chosen route, rejected alternatives and reasons, effective date, historical exposure, required filings, source links and the date the law/portal was checked.

The decision must separately address mandatory registration versus an optional voluntary registration under section 25(3), including business, pricing, invoicing, compliance and cancellation consequences. Voluntary registration is not automatically beneficial. If the facts do not establish the route, keep it unresolved and request more evidence rather than filing.

## Prepare the application only after the route is confirmed

GST Portal guidance describes a normal taxpayer application in FORM GST REG-01 with sections for business details, promoters/partners, authorised signatory, principal place of business, additional places, goods and services, state-specific details, Aadhaar authentication and verification. Prepare a checklist for the relevant entity type; not every document applies in every case.

Potential records the official portal checklist includes, depending on constitution and premises:

- Entity/PAN and proof of constitution (for example, incorporation/registration evidence, partnership deed or equivalent).
- Promoter/partner/director and authorised-signatory information, proof of appointment where applicable, and the required electronic verification method.
- Proof for the principal place of business; for owned or rented/consented premises, use the portal’s current accepted-document list for that possession type. Add documents for additional places only when applicable.
- Goods/services descriptions and relevant codes, state/jurisdiction details, and contact details for OTP verification.
- Bank-account details are optional in the REG-01 application flow, but after registration Rule 10A requires furnishing them within 30 days of grant or before furnishing outward-supply details in GSTR-1 / using IFF, whichever is earlier. Confirm the current portal workflow and deadline with the CA; failure can affect registration status.
- Track both furnishing and successful portal verification of the business bank account, including evidence that the portal shows it verified, within the practitioner-confirmed Rule 10A deadline. Do not record account numbers or bank documents in Personal HQ.
- State-specific or business-model documents requested by the current portal form.

For the principal place of business, CBIC Instruction 03/2025-GST gives document-handling guidance that is useful when assembling the secure file set. For owned premises, one accepted ownership document (such as a recent property-tax receipt, municipal khata or electricity bill) should generally establish ownership. For rented premises, prepare the valid rent/lease agreement and one accepted document establishing the lessor's ownership. The instruction says an unregistered agreement should also be accompanied by lessor identity proof; a registered agreement should not require that identity proof. If the tenant's electricity or water document is in the tenant's name, the instruction says that and the rent agreement should suffice. For consented premises, prepare the owner's consent, identity proof and one ownership document; use the current portal checklist for shared-premises cases. Ask a GST practitioner to resolve unusual property arrangements. Store all actual documents privately.

Use the GST Portal itself or a CA’s secure document channel for actual identifiers and files. Never put PAN, Aadhaar, OTPs, bank records, signed documents or tax credentials in this dashboard, a public GitHub repository, or a public Pages site. The dashboard should store only milestones and whether a checklist item is complete.

6. Ask the CA to confirm the effective date and treatment of any pre-registration/historical orders, invoice configuration, tax codes/rates, place-of-supply treatment, e-commerce/TCS flows, return types and due dates, input-credit records, e-invoice/e-way-bill applicability, and any display requirements that apply to ValueMela.
7. Verify the post-registration bank account on the portal, not merely its entry. Before each dispatch, operate the CA-confirmed e-way-bill/document check for the responsible seller, consignee or transporter, including applicable state rules and exceptions.
8. Add only verified recurring filings to the HQ with due dates and an owner; do not guess due dates or mark compliance complete without a filed-return acknowledgment.

## Registration review and clarification watch

CBIC Instruction 03/2025-GST says complete applications that are not flagged as risky should be processed within seven working days. For specified risk/Aadhaar-authentication cases, it directs grant within 30 days after physical verification. These are processing directions, not a promise of approval or a deadline for ValueMela's case. The same instruction describes when an officer may issue a FORM GST REG-03 clarification notice; where one arrives, track its actual portal due date and prepare the FORM GST REG-04 reply with the CA. The instruction says the applicant's reply is due within seven working days of receipt. Never put an ARN, OTP, PAN, Aadhaar, private address proof or signed application in Personal HQ.

Track every portal outcome privately: acknowledgment/ARN, grant and certificate verification, REG-03 clarification and REG-04 response, rejection and stated reasons, withdrawal, suspension/cancellation notice, portal error or missed deadline. For each outcome, have the CA identify the actual statutory/portal deadline, evidence needed, permitted correction, appeal or fresh-application route, responsible owner and proof of completion. Do not assume an application is approved because it was submitted or a processing window elapsed.
