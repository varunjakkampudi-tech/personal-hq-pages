### New e-commerce warehouse option to assess (effective 1 November 2026)

Notification 05/2026-Central Tax inserted CGST Rule 14B, generally effective 1 November 2026. It provides a conditional same-PAN regular registration option in another State/UT with no physical presence there, by declaring a consenting section 52 tax-collecting e-commerce operator warehouse as the principal place of business. It applies only to goods supplied through that operator. The specified monthly output tax liability on those supplies to registered recipients other than recipients on the same PAN must not exceed ₹2,50,000; this is a tax-liability test, not a ₹2.5 lakh turnover/sales cap. The rule also involves a home-State registration and REG-01 application, operator consent, biometric Aadhaar/document verification and physical verification at the home-State premises. Check the 18 November 2026 Gazette corrigendum before relying on the original English text: it corrects Rule 14B condition lettering, related rule/form cross-references and wording. This may be relevant only if ValueMela’s actual seller, goods, section 52 operator and warehouse flow satisfy the rule; it is not a general exemption or automatic route. Have the CA confirm ValueMela's eligibility, the corrected Gazette wording, portal implementation and practical obligations before choosing this route.

Have an Indian GST practitioner/CA confirm the registration trigger, state-wise registrations, business classification, product tax treatment, e-commerce provisions and scheme eligibility against the actual model and current law. For Rule 14B specifically, have the CA verify the corrected Gazette text and GST Portal implementation; establish whether the registered seller, goods, section 52 operator, consented warehouse and recipient/tax-liability facts meet each condition; and advise on home-State prerequisites, records, amendments, ongoing eligibility and withdrawal/cancellation steps before selecting the route. CBIC’s portal FAQs distinguish an electronic-commerce operator from a business selling only its own products and from suppliers selling through an operator; do not infer ValueMela’s classification from its brand name or website alone.

### Simplified registration option to compare: CGST Rule 14A

The current GST Portal REG-01 guide also asks applicants to choose whether they opt for Rule 14A. The GST Council's official November 2025 portal-update notice describes this as an optional route for applicants who self-assess that total output tax on supplies to registered persons (including CGST, SGST/UTGST, IGST and compensation cess) will not exceed ₹2,50,000 per month. Aadhaar authentication is required for the primary authorised signatory and at least one promoter/partner; the portal says the Rule 14A and Rule 14B options cannot be selected together. The portal's GSTR-1 guidance currently prevents summary generation when reported B2B tax exceeds the monthly cap and says the taxpayer must withdraw from Rule 14A to report without that restriction. A Rule 14A registration also has withdrawal conditions, including filing due returns and a minimum one tax period of returns when withdrawal is requested on or after 1 April 2026; check the current portal form and practitioner advice before relying on this summary.

Ask the practitioner to compare Rule 14A with ordinary registration and, only if the actual seller/warehouse facts qualify, Rule 14B. Record B2B mix and projected tax liability, Aadhaar readiness, number of same-PAN registrations per State/UT, filing/withdrawal constraints, likely growth beyond the cap and consequences for ValueMela's invoicing and customer mix. The ₹2.5 lakh figure is a tax-liability cap on relevant B2B supplies, not a turnover cap. Do not opt into a simplified route merely because the portal offers it; confirm the current rule text and ongoing obligations for the actual model.

## Prepare the application only after the route is confirmed

GST Portal guidance describes a normal taxpayer application in FORM GST REG-01 with sections for business details, promoters/partners, authorised signatory, principal place of business, additional places, goods and services, state-specific details, Aadhaar authentication and verification. Prepare a checklist for the relevant entity type; not every document applies in every case.

Potential records the official portal checklist includes, depending on constitution and premises:

- Entity/PAN and proof of constitution (for example, incorporation/registration evidence, partnership deed or equivalent).
- Promoter/partner/director and authorised-signatory information, proof of appointment where applicable, and the required electronic verification method.
- Proof for the principal place of business; for owned or rented/consented premises, use the portal’s current accepted-document list for that possession type. Add documents for additional places only when applicable.
- Goods/services descriptions and relevant codes, state/jurisdiction details, and contact details for OTP verification.
- The GST Portal normal-taxpayer REG-01 guide now documents a Bank Accounts tab introduced in November 2026: furnishing account details during the application is optional, and up to 10 business accounts linked with the applicant PAN may be added. This does not remove the post-grant Rule 10A requirement: furnish bank-account details within 30 days of grant or before furnishing outward-supply details in GSTR-1 / using IFF, whichever is earlier. Confirm the exact applicant-specific portal workflow and deadline with the practitioner; verify successful account validation, not merely entry. The live GST Portal guide can change.
- Track both furnishing and successful portal verification of the business bank account within the practitioner-confirmed Rule 10A deadline. Keep account numbers and bank documents out of Personal HQ.
- State-specific or business-model documents requested by the current portal form.

For the principal place of business, CBIC Instruction 03/2025-GST gives document-handling guidance that is useful when assembling the secure file set. For owned premises, one accepted ownership document (such as a recent property-tax receipt, municipal khata or electricity bill) should generally establish ownership. For rented premises, prepare the valid rent/lease agreement and one accepted document establishing the lessor's ownership. The instruction says an unregistered agreement should also be accompanied by lessor identity proof; a registered agreement should not require that identity proof. If the tenant's electricity or water document is in the tenant's name, the instruction says that and the rent agreement should suffice. For consented premises, prepare the owner's consent, identity proof and one ownership document; use the current portal checklist for shared-premises cases. Ask a GST practitioner to resolve unusual property arrangements. Store all actual documents privately.

Use the GST Portal itself or a CA’s secure document channel for actual identifiers and files. Never put PAN, Aadhaar, OTPs, bank records, signed documents or tax credentials in this dashboard, a public GitHub repository, or a public Pages site. The dashboard should store only milestones and whether a checklist item is complete.

6. Ask the CA to confirm effective date and pre-registration/historical-order treatment, invoice configuration, tax codes/rates, place-of-supply treatment, e-commerce/TCS flows, return types and due dates, input-credit records, e-invoice/e-way-bill applicability, and any display requirements that apply to ValueMela.
7. Verify the post-registration bank account on the portal, not merely its entry. Operate the CA-confirmed e-way-bill/document check before dispatch, including applicable state rules and exceptions.
7. Add only verified recurring filings to the HQ with due dates and an owner; do not guess due dates or mark compliance complete without a filed-return acknowledgment.

## Registration review and clarification watch

CBIC Instruction 03/2025-GST says complete applications that are not flagged as risky should be processed within seven working days. For specified risk/Aadhaar-authentication cases, it directs grant within 30 days after physical verification. These are processing directions, not a promise of approval or a deadline for ValueMela's case. The same instruction describes when an officer may issue a FORM GST REG-03 clarification notice; where one arrives, track its actual portal due date and prepare the FORM GST REG-04 reply with the CA. The instruction says the applicant's reply is due within seven working days of receipt. Never put an ARN, OTP, PAN, Aadhaar, private address proof or signed application in Personal HQ.
